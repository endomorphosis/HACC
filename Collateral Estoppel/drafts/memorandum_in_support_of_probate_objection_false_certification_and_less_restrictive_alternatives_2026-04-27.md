## MEMORANDUM IN SUPPORT OF BENJAMIN BARBER'S OBJECTION TO FIRST AMENDED PETITION, REQUEST FOR HEARING, AND REQUEST TO DENY OR NARROW EMERGENCY, PLENARY, WRIT, AND FEE RELIEF

Objector Benjamin Barber submits this memorandum in support of his objection to Solomon Barber's First Amended Petition for Appointment of Guardian, waiver-of-notice request, and writ-of-assistance request.

This memorandum also supports Objector's separate ORCP 17 sanctions motion, but it does not ask the probate court to make a criminal perjury finding. The point for this Court is narrower and probate-specific: the petition was declared true for use as evidence in court, yet it contains statements and omissions that Objector contends were false, materially misleading, unsupported, or made without reasonable inquiry. Those alleged defects matter because Solomon used them to request emergency control over Jane Kay Cortez, waiver of prior notice, plenary authority, fees from Jane's funds, and law-enforcement transport.

## I. Summary

Solomon's petition asks the Court to treat him as the emergency solution to conditions that he allegedly helped create or worsen.

The petition tells the Court that Jane faces immediate danger because Benjamin recently began living with her, failed to provide housing documentation, caused voucher failure, interfered with APS and police welfare checks, isolated Jane, and left Jane with no alternative housing. It then asks for waiver of prior notice, temporary and indefinite guardianship, plenary authority, attorney fees from Jane's funds, and a writ authorizing law enforcement to enter Jane's home and transport her to a hospital.

Objector contends that framing is materially incomplete and misleading. The record already shows or supports the following:

1. Jane obtained a restraining order against Solomon in case `25PO11530`, including personal-contact and property-control restrictions.
2. The restraining-order packet states that the order was granted, in effect, and that violation may be contempt.
3. Solomon knew of the order and later asserted that he was not bound until service occurred on his terms.
4. The order included restrictions on exercising control over Jane's money or property, yet Solomon's probate petition sought broad practical control over Jane's person, housing, records, and possible inheritance.
5. Solomon allegedly communicated with or used HACC/housing channels concerning Jane and Benjamin while the restraining-order dispute was active.
6. Jane and Benjamin deny authorizing Solomon to remove Benjamin from the lease, household composition, approved-occupant status, or HACC housing paperwork.
7. Benjamin has declared that Jane raised him and that he had lived with Jane for more than six years, directly contradicting the petition's recent-entry narrative.
8. Solomon was not raised by Jane, had not lived with Jane, and is not a neutral caretaker; Objector contends he is an estranged son whose sudden request for control must be evaluated against the inheritance and trust issues raised in the same petition.
9. A current hotel reservation at University Place Hotel provides a concrete short-term bridge plan beginning April 30, 2026, and undercuts the premise that the only immediate option is emergency guardianship, law-enforcement transport, or plenary control.

For these reasons, Objector asks the Court to deny or sharply narrow the emergency, waiver-of-notice, plenary-authority, writ, and fee requests; require Solomon to identify the source for each disputed allegation; and set an evidentiary hearing before relying on the challenged narrative.

## II. The Petition Was Used As Evidence And Carries A False-Certification Problem

The First Amended Petition was not a casual letter. It was filed in court, used to request emergency relief, and declared true for use as evidence under penalty of perjury. The OCR of the petition reflects that Solomon declared:

> "I HEREBY DECLARE THAT THE ABOVE STATEMENT IS TRUE TO THE BEST OF MY KNOWLEDGE AND BELIEF, AND THAT I UNDERSTAND THAT IT IS MADE FOR USE AS EVIDENCE IN COURT AND IS SUBJECT TO PENALTY FOR PERJURY."

The Court therefore should not treat disputed factual assertions as harmless background. The challenged assertions were central to the requested relief.

Oregon law also recognizes the seriousness of knowingly false written statements to public officials. ORS 162.085 provides that a person commits unsworn falsification if the person knowingly makes a false written statement to a public servant in connection with an application for a benefit. Objector does not ask this Court to make a criminal ruling under ORS 162.085. Objector cites that statute only to explain why a knowingly false unsworn court submission seeking court-conferred authority is a serious matter and why the probate court should require a clean evidentiary record before granting extraordinary relief.

For probate purposes, ORCP 17 is the primary vehicle. ORS 125.050 makes the Oregon Rules of Civil Procedure and Oregon Evidence Code applicable in protective proceedings except as otherwise provided by ORS chapter 125. ORCP 17 requires a reasonable inquiry before a party or attorney presents factual assertions, legal positions, or requests for relief to the Court. ORCP 17 also gives the Court authority, after notice and an opportunity to be heard, to impose corrective or deterrent sanctions for false certifications. Separately, ORS 40.135 / OEC 311 permits an adverse presumption if the Court finds willful suppression of evidence.

ORS chapter 125 independently requires narrowed protective relief. ORS 125.305 requires clear and convincing evidence that guardianship conditions are established, that the appointment is necessary for continuing care and supervision, and that the nominated person is qualified, suitable, and willing to serve. ORS 125.305 also requires a guardianship order to be no more restrictive on the protected person's liberty than reasonably necessary.

The temporary-guardianship provisions make the emergency request even narrower. ORS 125.600 requires a specific finding by clear and convincing evidence that there is an immediate and serious danger to the respondent's life or health and that the respondent's welfare requires immediate action. ORS 125.600 also limits a temporary fiduciary to a specific purpose and a specific period of time. ORS 125.605 requires notice before appointment unless immediate and serious danger requires immediate appointment, requires a visitor if a temporary guardian is sought, and requires objections to a temporary fiduciary to be heard within two judicial days after filing.

ORS 125.080 further supports hearing and counsel protections. Where an objection is made or filed, the Court should not proceed on a one-sided emergency narrative without ensuring Jane Kay Cortez has any counsel required by statute, or confirming that counsel is unnecessary only because Jane is already represented or objects to appointment of counsel.

## III. Probate-Law Objections Independent Of Sanctions

Even if the Court reserves ORCP 17 sanctions for later notice and hearing, the same record defects require immediate probate relief.

First, the Court should not grant temporary guardianship or waiver of notice unless Solomon proves the statutory emergency predicates by clear and convincing evidence on a clean record. The challenged petition narrative is not clean: it omits the restraining order, omits Solomon's alleged role in housing/property interference, omits HACC/Quantum delay, misdescribes or overstates Benjamin's residence/incarceration history, and omits less restrictive alternatives.

Second, any temporary authority must be specific, time-limited, and tied to demonstrated need. The petition requested temporary and indefinite guardianship, plenary authority, residence/placement authority, fees from Jane's funds, and law-enforcement transport. That combination is broader than the record presently supports.

Third, any order should preserve Jane's self-determination, supportive relationships, housing stability, privacy, and property rights. ORS 125.315 requires a guardian to promote the protected person's self-determination, involve the protected person in decisions to the extent practicable, identify the protected person's values and preferences, and make reasonable efforts to identify and facilitate supportive relationships and services. A petition that omits Jane's restraining-order posture against Solomon and seeks to remove or control her through Solomon is in tension with those duties.

Relatedly, the Court should not allow the guardianship petition to become an indirect association or placement order. ORS 125.320 imposes limitations and notice procedures before an adult protected person is placed in a mental health treatment facility, nursing home, or other residential facility. ORS 125.323 separately protects a protected person's preferred associations and provides a procedure for challenging unreasonable association limits. Those protections matter here because the petition seeks placement-related authority and portrays Benjamin's relationship with Jane as isolation without fairly presenting Jane's preferences, the restraining-order context, or the six-year household relationship.

Fourth, if the Court believes any protective intervention is needed, the least restrictive immediate path is not Solomon's plenary control. The Court can instead set a prompt evidentiary hearing, require a visitor report, require source identification and production, appoint or confirm independent counsel for Jane if required, preserve the University Place reservation as a short-term bridge, and enter noninterference orders protecting Jane's housing, records, service-animal access, privacy, and property.

## IV. Challenged Statements And Current Refutations

Objector's separate ORCP 17 sanctions motion identifies the false or materially misleading certifications with specificity. For purposes of this objection memorandum, the same allegations matter because they should not be used as the factual foundation for emergency, plenary, writ, fee, or waiver-of-notice relief.

### A. "Incarcerated Until Recently" And "Begun Living With" Jane

The petition portrayed Benjamin as recently incarcerated and as having only recently begun living with Jane in violation of occupancy terms.

Objector refutes that framing. Benjamin has declared that Jane raised him continually since birth and that he had lived with Jane for more than six years. The current record also indicates that the incarceration reference involved an older nonviolent misdemeanor history, not a recent release creating an emergency guardianship basis.

This matters because the petition used the recent-entry narrative to make Benjamin appear to be a new danger or destabilizing stranger in Jane's household. That narrative supported emergency framing, waiver of notice, and coercive relief.

### B. Housing Documentation, Voucher Failure, And "Force The Courts To Evict Him"

The petition alleged that Benjamin failed to provide housing documentation, said he would force courts to evict him if needed, and caused Jane to be unable to obtain a voucher.

Objector refutes that one-cause narrative. The record reflects a materially more complex HACC / Quantum / relocation timeline, including Benjamin's repeated document submissions, Quantum or HACC packet-routing problems, HACC internal-review and household-composition decisions, a disputed lease-removal sequence, and later restoration or recognition of Benjamin in housing notices. Objector also contends that any statement about using court process was mischaracterized: insisting on lawful court process during a housing and restraining-order dispute is not the same as causing Jane's voucher failure.

This matters because the housing-causation narrative was used to support urgency, guardianship authority, possible removal or placement authority, and fee-shifting against Jane's funds.

The petition also omitted the federal relocation framework. If the HACC displacement path was a Section 18 demolition or disposition relocation, 24 CFR 970.21 required 90-day notice before displacement and comparable housing, and tenant-based assistance such as a voucher was not treated as comparable housing until actual relocation. To the extent another federally assisted relocation framework applies, 49 CFR 24.203 separately supports the same basic point that lawful occupants are not simply required to move without notice and comparable replacement housing. Objector uses those authorities carefully: the point is not that every voucher dispute creates a universal 90-day bar, but that Solomon's petition materially oversimplified the housing and court-process issues.

### C. Omission Of The Restraining-Order And Records-Production Context

The petition sought broad protective control over Jane while omitting the restraining-order case involving Solomon.

Objector refutes the omission as material. The restraining-order materials show Jane as petitioner and Solomon as respondent. The order included contact, residence-entry, and money/property-control restrictions. Solomon had notice of the dispute and later took the position that he did not have to comply until service occurred on his terms. The petition did not fairly disclose that context while asking the probate court for authority over Jane's person, housing, records, placement, and possible property interests.

This matters because a petition seeking waiver of notice, plenary authority, fees from Jane's funds, and law-enforcement transport should not omit a contemporaneous restraining-order framework involving the proposed guardian.

### D. No Less Restrictive Alternative, No Trustee, No Attorney-In-Fact, And No Existing Authority

The petition alleged in substance that there was no less restrictive alternative and that Jane had no trustee, attorney-in-fact, health-care representative, or relevant existing authority.

Objector refutes that as unsupported or materially incomplete. The record already raised trust, estate, representative-payee, HACC authority, and claimed power-of-attorney issues. Solomon allegedly failed or refused to produce the records needed to test those claims. The University Place reservation also now supplies a concrete short-term housing alternative that was not consistent with the petition's "nowhere else to go" emergency framing.

Objector does not ask the Court to treat exact estate values, acreage, or timber-lease values as established until direct source pages are produced and authenticated. The present point is narrower: Solomon invoked trust, inheritance, representative-payee, and financial-control issues while presenting a no-alternative narrative without producing the complete records needed to test it.

### E. APS, Police Welfare Checks, Isolation, And Immediate Danger

The petition alleged that Benjamin interfered with APS or police welfare checks, isolated Jane, and created immediate and serious danger.

Objector refutes that framing as materially incomplete. The petition omitted that Jane was seeking restraining-order protection against Solomon during the same protective-order sequence and that Benjamin raised court-process and conflict concerns. Jane's later declaration materials also contradict the idea that law-enforcement-assisted removal was plainly protective and undisputed.

This matters because the APS/police and isolation allegations were used to justify extraordinary relief, including waiver of prior notice, emergency appointment, and writ-based transport.

### F. Writ-Based Transport, Plenary Authority, And Fees From Jane's Funds

The petition sought law-enforcement assistance to enter Jane's home and transport her, sought plenary authority, and requested payment of fees and costs from Jane's funds.

Objector refutes the necessity and benefit of that relief on the current record. The same petition rested on disputed housing blame, omitted restraining-order context, omitted or minimized less restrictive alternatives, and failed to provide a clean source record for the allegations that supposedly justified coercive relief. Fees incurred to pursue false, unsupported, materially misleading, overbroad, or coercive relief should not be charged to Jane's SSI/SSA, representative-payee funds, trust funds, inheritance, or property.

This matters because ORS 125.098 requires attention to whether services benefited Jane. On a record this disputed, the Court should deny or reserve fee shifting unless Solomon proves a concrete benefit to Jane and a lawful basis for charging her funds.
